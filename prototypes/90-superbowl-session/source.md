# PROTOTYPE #90 — the words of a Dutch match report

Source text for the Oracle's `scratch` notebook. Deleted when the prototype ends.

A Turkish-speaking learner of Dutch, B1 level, is studying the vocabulary of a short news
report about a sports match. These are the words, their Turkish meanings, and the memory
scene (Hook), word-split (Split) or false-friend warning (Trap) attached to each.

- **de wedstrijd** — maç, match. Split: `wed` (bahis / wager) + `strijd` (mücadele / struggle).
  Hook: the last minute of a match, a packed stand rising to its feet from the stress.
- **de ploeg** — takım, team. Trap: the same Dutch word also means a farmer's plough.
  Hook: eleven plugs in one extension lead; pull one out and the whole team loses power.
- **winnen** — kazanmak, to win. Bridge: English `win`, same Germanic root.
- **verliezen** — kaybetmek, to lose. Bridge: English `forlorn` = Dutch `verloren`.
- **verslaan** — yenmek, to defeat. Split: `ver-` (completive prefix) + `slaan` (to hit).
- **de uitslag** — sonuç, result. Split: `uit` (out) + `slag` (stroke). Trap: the same word
  means an election result AND a skin rash.
- **de helft** — yarı, half. Bridge: English `half`. Trap: Dutch splits the English word
  `half` into an adjective `half` and a noun `de helft`.
- **de aanval** — hücum, attack. Split: `aan` (on) + `val` (fall, from `vallen`).
- **de verdediging** — savunma, defence. No split: `dedig-` is not a Dutch word.
- **de scheidsrechter** — hakem, referee. Split: `scheid` (to separate) + `-s-` + `rechter`
  (judge). Literally: the judge who separates.
- **de toeschouwer** — seyirci, spectator. Split: `toe` + `schouw` (to look) + `-er`.
- **het optreden** — gösteri, performance. Split: `op` (up) + `treden` (to step).
- **de pauze** — devre arası, half-time break.
- **de titel** — şampiyonluk, title.
- **het record** — rekor, record.
- **de verlenging** — uzatma, extra time. Split: `ver-` + `leng` (from `lang`, long) + `-ing`.

## The word order of a Dutch news sentence

`De Seattle Seahawks wonnen zondag in Santa Clara de zestigste Super Bowl.`

who — verb (always second) — when — where — what. Time always comes before place.
