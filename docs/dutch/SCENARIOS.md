# Scenarios

Recurring situations in the learner's life that need Dutch. Docent reads this library to dress a Session when the learner types no Theme and no intake issue is open — the third and last step of the Theme priority locked by [#83](https://github.com/atilileri/atilileri.github.io/issues/83).

A Scenario is **never consumed**. It is used, its `lastUsed` date is updated, and it stays in the library. The library is **capped at 30**: adding the thirty-first means retiring one.

A Scenario **links to** Plan objectives and never states an objective of its own. That is the boundary against [`PLAN.md`](./PLAN.md).

**The objective field is prose today.** [The Plan](https://github.com/atilileri/atilileri.github.io/issues/85) does not exist yet, so no Objective id exists. Each Scenario therefore carries one plain-language `objective` sentence marked `unmapped`. It is **not a key**: no agent may resolve it, follow it, or treat it as an id. When the Plan ships its Objectives, every `unmapped` sentence here is replaced by real Objective ids, one edit per Scenario.

**Register** is one of three values: `informal` (`je`; friends, teammates, a market stall), `neutral` (`je` or `u`; a shop, a class, a stranger), `formal` (`u`; official letters, a doctor, a bank).

**How this file grows.** Docent proposes a Scenario at the end of a Session and the learner confirms it. Docent never writes this file silently.

## `padel-app` — The padel WhatsApp groups

- **Setting**: Two Dutch-language WhatsApp groups for padel at Peakz Padel in Eindhoven. Players arrange matches, swap places, cancel at short notice and joke between themselves. The Dutch is fast, written, abbreviated and full of slang.
- **Register**: `informal`
- **Objective** (`unmapped`): Read fast informal written Dutch well enough to know what is being arranged, and reply in one short line.
- **Last used**: never

## `zaterdagmarkt` — The Saturday market in Eindhoven

- **Setting**: The weekly market, most Saturdays when the weather is good. Short spoken exchanges at a stall: asking for a quantity, asking a price, being told a total, answering a stallholder's small talk.
- **Register**: `informal`
- **Objective** (`unmapped`): Complete a short spoken purchase in Dutch without switching to English, including numbers, quantities and money.
- **Last used**: never

## `borden-en-billboards` — Signs, billboards and notices

- **Setting**: The Dutch that is simply there — traffic signs on the cycle route to the office, shop notices, station announcements in print, advertising along the road. No conversation, no stakes, and available every day.
- **Register**: `neutral`
- **Objective** (`unmapped`): Read a public sign or a short public notice and act on it correctly, including warnings and prohibitions.
- **Last used**: never

## `post-in-de-bus` — Post in the mailbox

- **Setting**: What arrives at home each week: the Groot Eindhoven newsletter, and letters from government bodies and service providers. The learner currently translates these rather than reading them. Official Dutch, long sentences, formal address, and a deadline hidden somewhere in it.
- **Register**: `formal`
- **Objective** (`unmapped`): Read a formal letter or a local news item and extract what is being asked, by when, and what happens otherwise.
- **Last used**: never

## `taalcafe-veldhoven` — The taal café at Veldhoven library

- **Setting**: A weekly Dutch conversation evening on Tuesdays at Veldhoven library, near the office. Strangers of mixed levels, no assessment, no consequence for failing. This is the learner's only planned speaking outlet, and the only Scenario where they must speak first.
- **Register**: `neutral`
- **Objective** (`unmapped`): Introduce oneself, hold a few turns of small talk with a stranger, and ask for repetition without switching to English.
- **Last used**: never
