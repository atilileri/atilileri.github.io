In the video **"AI Coding is exhausting,"** creator Matt Pocock discusses the psychological and cognitive shift that AI tools have introduced to software development.

Here is a detailed breakdown of his explanation for why coding with AI feels so mentally draining:

### The Loss of "Mental Breaks"

Before AI tools became mainstream, traditional programming involved a mix of high-level design and low-level typing. Writing the actual code—the "tactical side"—often served as a mental break for developers [[00:17](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3De-pFrQ_Rh0s%26t%3D17)]. Pocock compares this type of rote implementation to a game of non-verbal reasoning where you are just moving pieces around without having to overthink.

### The Shift to Constant Strategic Thinking

AI has completely consumed the tactical side of programming [[00:36](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3De-pFrQ_Rh0s%26t%3D36)]. Because the AI now handles the "boring grunt work" of typing out the boilerplate and implementation details, human developers are forced to constantly operate at a level *above* the AI. Instead of typing, developers spend their time reviewing, directing, and architecting. Everything a developer does now requires long-term, strategic thinking, which demands significantly more continuous mental energy [[00:00](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3De-pFrQ_Rh0s%26t%3D0)].

### The "Lead Developer" Effect

Because the AI is acting as the junior developer writing the code, every human programmer using these tools has essentially been promoted to a "lead developer" role [[00:48](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3De-pFrQ_Rh0s%26t%3D48)]. While skipping the grunt work to focus on high-level decisions is enjoyable, being constantly locked into this higher-level managerial and architectural mindset puts the brain under immense strain [[01:06](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3De-pFrQ_Rh0s%26t%3D66)].

Ultimately, Pocock notes that while he genuinely likes making these high-level decisions, the constant strategic strain means he ends every day feeling completely exhausted [[01:10](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3De-pFrQ_Rh0s%26t%3D70)].
