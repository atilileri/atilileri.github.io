I have never owned a motorcycle. I have never held the licence for one. So this
is not a rider's account of the open road — it is the view from the queue,
written by somebody sitting in a car watching a bike filter past.

### The practical case comes first

Start with the boring argument, because the boring argument is the one that
actually holds. A motorcycle takes a fraction of the space of a car, on the road
and once it is parked. It gets through traffic that a car simply sits in. In a
country built around bicycles, where cities already assume two wheels, a small
engine between two wheels looks less like a toy and more like an obvious tool.

Every time I lose twenty minutes to a queue that a rider slips through, the
argument gets one notch stronger.

### And then there is the other reason

The practical case would be enough. It is not the whole reason, and pretending
otherwise would be dishonest.

There is something genuinely appealing about the exposure. A car is a room you
move around in; a bike is not a room at all. You feel the temperature change
when the road drops into a valley. You smell the countryside instead of
recirculating it. People who ride describe the difference in terms that sound
overblown until you notice they all describe it the same way.

I suspect the appeal and the practicality are the same fact seen from two
angles. The thing that makes a bike efficient — that there is almost nothing to
it — is also the thing that makes it feel different.

### What is actually stopping me

Nothing dramatic. Time, mostly, and the ordinary friction of starting something
that has a syllabus, a test and a queue for the test.

That is a bad reason, and I know it. Learning to kitesurf had the same shape:
months of thinking about it, then a week of doing it, then wondering what the
delay had been for. Skiing was the same last year. The pattern is consistent
enough that I should probably stop treating it as new information.

So: not yet, but the licence is on the list. When it happens I will write the
other version of this post — the one from the saddle, where I find out how much
of this was right.
